# Matching, dialogue, and outreach

Matching accepts only a Zod-validated `ConfirmedProfile`. Registry retrieval uses Traditional Chinese confirmed labels for TFDA and English confirmed labels for ClinicalTrials.gov; this preserves bilingual recall while each assessment still traces to the same fact IDs.

The engine evaluates condition wording, recruitment status, age, sex, location, and eligibility details. The compact matrix shows the five useful repeated comparisons and omits the generic “Other” block that otherwise repeats uncertain across cards. The four outcomes are `possibly_met` (green/aligned), `possibly_not_met` (red/different), `unknown` (yellow/uncertain), and `missing` (gray/missing information). Color meaning is available in the legend and each block exposes its criterion, outcome, and rationale on pointer hover or keyboard focus. Meaningful eligibility differences and missing details remain in the expanded evidence. Overall labels are limited to `discuss`, `needs_review`, `needs_information`, and `unlikely_based_on_public_record`; the product never returns `eligible`.

Condition overlap is only a terminology intersection between the patient-confirmed summary and public registry wording. Eligibility details remain uncertain until reviewed criterion by criterion; the interface must not convert text overlap into an eligibility decision. Every card separately shows the patient-confirmed disease, histologic subtype, stage, biomarker, and age used as context, including explicit missing labels.

Before results are displayed, the client inspects up to twelve candidate public records, deduplicates missing high-signal domains, and asks at most six source-traceable follow-up questions. A person must answer or explicitly choose unknown; each answer is itself the confirmation, so there is no second bulk-confirmation checkbox. Unknown answers do not become profile facts. Other responses are identifier-masked, added as confirmed structured facts, and the comparison is rerun before any result card appears.

With visible WebMCP permission, `review_trial_followups` can read this pending question list so a browser agent can explain what is blocking comparison and why. The tool returns questions and a next step only. It has no answer parameter and cannot modify or confirm the profile; answers remain in the visible human form.

Results are grouped as no known public-record difference, missing information, and public-record differences. The last group is collapsed by default. Card and compact-list views expose the same source details. When a confirmed prior or current treatment term also appears in an explicit public exclusion section, the result shows a potential exclusion signal and moves to the public-difference group. Term overlap does not establish exclusion; treatment name, timing, and context still require the study team.

Agent mode keeps a persistent right-side assistant throughout the workflow. Before confirmation, every guided message is browser-masked and sent with only the workflow stage plus minimized state; the returned workflow action is schema-bounded and cannot confirm a medical fact unless the person explicitly states they reviewed all visible facts. Manual mode does not send guided-chat messages. Result dialogue requires a purpose-specific checkbox and receives only fact `id/domain/value`, the question, and up to five minimized public trial summaries. Thinking output is disabled and responses are not persisted by the application.

Outreach is created locally from confirmed facts and public registry information. It always includes `sent: false`; there is no send, email, message, enrollment, booking, or consent endpoint.

The optional care-team discussion brief is also created locally and includes at most five current comparisons. It has two linked sections: a clinician-facing registry brief and a plain-language patient/family handout. The brief inventories confirmed context, comparison states, potential exclusion signals, source URLs and retrieval dates, missing information, and questions to ask. Registry listings are explicitly classified as research plans rather than efficacy evidence; benefit, harms, evidence quality, and guideline alignment remain `not assessed`. The downloaded Markdown contains confirmed health information, carries an in-product storage warning, and is never uploaded or sent by TrialBridge TW.
